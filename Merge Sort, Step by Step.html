The idea that makes it work


Merging two lists that are already sorted is easy. The smallest remaining item must be at the front of one of them. So you compare the two front items, take the smaller one, and repeat. When one side runs out, copy the rest of the other.


A list with one item is already sorted. That gives the splitting a place to stop, and every merge after it starts with two sorted halves.


Stability: on a tie, the code takes from the left (<=), so equal values keep their original order. The default list has two 27s, marked a and b. Watch 27a stay ahead of 27b.


Why it costs n log n


Halving a list of n items takes about log₂ n levels to reach single items. The merges on each level handle at most n items in total. Multiply the two to get O(n log n).
